**OVERHEARD ON REDDIT**
**Can the FIE tell you where not to fence?**

*The debate this fall on r/Fencing:* the world federation's warning about the new World Fencing League set off a 50-comment argument.

**What the FIE actually said.** According to the South China Morning Post, a statement signed by FIE Secretary General Gulnora Saidova on Oct. 22 said the league is not an official competition, and none of its planned events is sanctioned. It pointed to rules that allow bans. (We couldn't find the full text posted publicly.) The rule, Article 1.2.6(b), bars federation members from events run by anyone "not recognised by the national federation of their country," unless that federation specially authorizes them.

**What the WFL is.** It's a made-for-TV team league started by U.S. Olympian Miles Chamley-Watson. Its one event so far, in Los Angeles in April, offered a $100,000 prize pool. In épée, a 45-second clock punishes passive fencing.

**Opinion: it's overreach.** Running your own championships is fine, but banning members from someone else's show isn't. And "event" is never defined.

**Opinion: you signed up for it.** Joining a federation means accepting its rules. The FIE is worried about rivals, not club tournaments.

**What it means for club fencers.** By the rule's own wording, USA Fencing tournaments and events run by recognized clubs are unaffected. One wrinkle: USA Fencing itself signed a partnership with the WFL in January, and it hasn't commented publicly.

*Fact check:* Seven weeks before the warning, the FIE hosted Chamley-Watson in Lausanne for "positive discussions."
