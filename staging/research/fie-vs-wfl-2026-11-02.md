## 2. What the World Fencing League is

- Founded in 2025 by Miles Chamley-Watson, a US Olympian (2016 Olympic team foil bronze, per SCMP). It is "a Chiron Sports Group company." https://worldfencingleague.org/about
- **Debut:** April 25, 2026 at the The Shrine in Los Angeles, streamed on DAZN, with 12 fencers on two mixed teams (Blade and Shield) and a $100,000 prize pool (WFL site; SCMP).
  - Team Shield won 5 of 6 fights.
  - Ryan Choi (HKG) beat Chamley-Watson 16-13 and won US$10,000 for that fight. https://www.scmp.com/sport/hong-kong/article/3351454/hong-kongs-ryan-choi-shines-los-angeles-world-fencing-league-debut
  - The épée fencers were Koki Kano, Gergely Siklósi, Eszter Muhari and Alexandra Ndolo (Wikipedia roster; secondary).
- **Format** (https://worldfencingleague.org/event-format): six individual fights decided on "effective fencing time" (foil 2 min, épée 3 min, sabre 1 min). Each fight won earns a team point, with sudden death if the teams are tied.
  - Épée: "No right of way - whoever hits first scores. If both hit within 40ms, both score." A "45-second passivity clock awards a point to the holder of the Advantage Marker."
  - Other changes: off-target hits don't stop the action, close-quarters fencing is allowed, and a bout is halted only when both feet leave the piste.
- **Next events:**
  - SCMP (Oct 16, 2026), citing sources: a Hong Kong event pencilled in for the penultimate week of April 2027, venue unconfirmed (Kai Tak Arena favoured). "The new event has not been sanctioned by the sport's world governing body, the FIE." https://www.scmp.com/sport/hong-kong/article/3371151/world-fencing-league-bringing-sports-best-and-brightest-hong-kong-april
  - SCMP (Oct 23): an "exclusive invitational exhibition in Hong Kong in January" with Cheung Ka-long and others.
- **Partnerships:**
  - USA Fencing signed a strategic-partnership MOU with the WFL on Jan 7, 2026. https://www.usafencing.org/news/2026/january/07/usa-fencing-world-fencing-league-sign-mou-designed-to-grow-the-sport-across-the-u-s-and-around-the-world
    - CEO Phil Andrews: "We're committed to doing what we can to see this initiative succeed, and we believe its success will be good for fencing at every level."
  - British Fencing signed an MOU on Aug 12, 2026. https://www.britishfencing.com/british-fencing-signs-partnership-with-the-world-fencing-league/
- **WFL response to the FIE:** none found. Nothing about it is on the WFL newsroom (https://worldfencingleague.org/news), and no quoted response appears in SCMP. **Unknown.**

## 3. The rule: FIE Statutes Art. 1.2.6(b), confirmed

Source: FIE Statutes, April 2026 edition, English, p. 7 (https://static.fie.org/uploads/39/196968-FIE%20Statutes%20ang.pdf, linked from https://fie.org/documents/statutes-and-admin-rules).

> 1.2.6 b) All members of member federations are forbidden from taking part in competitions or events organised by an individual or entity which is not recognised by the national federation of their country, unless these competitions or events have been specially authorised by that same federation.
>
> International competitions which are not organised by a member federation and in which there are more than five nationalities must have the approval of the FIE and must appear on the International Calendar.

- Art. 1.2.6 itself states no penalty. Penalties come from the disciplinary chapter, Art. 7.1.4: warning, censure, disqualification, suspension ("deprives the defendant of all participation in the activities, sporting or other, organised under the aegis of the FIE, of the zonal confederations or the member federations"), expulsion and fines.
- Also relevant, Art. 8.1.1(b)(vii): a fencer may not "enter a competition organised only by a professional body, but he may take part in mixed competitions (with or against professionals) as long this is authorised by the FIE".
- Reddit's "it has existed for a long time" claim: the clause is in the current statutes, but I did not verify how old it is. **Unknown.**
- Reddit's "competition is not defined" claim: plausible, but I did not verify it across the whole document. Don't print it as fact.

## 4. USA Fencing and US youth fencers

- No USA Fencing statement on the FIE warning was found. **Unknown.**
- What we know: USA Fencing is an official WFL partner (Jan 2026 MOU). It also signed an MOU with the FIE in April 2026: https://www.usafencing.org/news/2026/april/28/usa-fencing-and-fie-sign-memorandum-of-understanding-designed-to-maximize-the-sport-s-moment-on-the-road-to-la28
- Practical reading, from the rule's text and not from any official guidance:
  - 1.2.6(b) targets events run by people or bodies the national federation does *not* recognise.
  - USA Fencing-sanctioned local, regional and national events, and tournaments run by USA Fencing member clubs, are recognised by definition.
  - Nothing in the statement as reported mentions domestic events.
  - Whether USA Fencing treats the WFL as "recognised" or "specially authorised", given its MOU, is **unknown**.
- No reported sanction of any fencer for WFL participation was found in press coverage.

## 5. Precedent and press

- SCMP draws the swimming comparison. The ISL claimed in 2018 that FINA (now World Aquatics) led a boycott that forced an event's cancellation, and "Earlier this year, the ISL was awarded a US$1 settlement in a US lawsuit over anti-trust issues… It had been asking for US$41 million in damages." That figure is as reported by SCMP; I did not check it against court records.
- The thread cites *International Skating Union v Commission*, C-124/21 P. Verified: the CJEU ruled on Dec 21, 2023 that the ISU's prior-authorisation and eligibility rules (bans for skating in unapproved events) breached EU competition law. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A62021CJ0124
  - No press source applies it to the FIE. If it prints, frame it as a commenter's comparison.
- Other coverage: Inside the Games (insidethegames.biz) has pre-launch coverage only. An aggregator, wearehealthy.org, dates the statement Oct 22. No Inside the Games story on the FIE warning was found.

## 6. Thread summary (opinions, paraphrased, no usernames)

**Overreach camp (most of the thread):**
- Running your own championships is one thing; claiming you can stop members fencing anywhere else is another.
- It's fair not to count outside results, but banning participation is wrong.
- The rule is so broad it could technically cover a club scrimmage, HEMA or SCA events, or (jokingly) a chili cook-off. "Competition" and "event" aren't defined.
- It's monopoly power. Some say it made them root for the WFL.
- It forces top athletes into a hard choice between prize money and an international career.
- EU competition law or the ISU skating case might make it unenforceable (opinion; the odds are uncertain even to those commenters).
- If the FIE wants exclusivity, it should pay fencers. Just ignore the WFL; this was an "unforced error."

**"You signed up for this" camp:**
- Joining a national federation means accepting its statutes. The comparison offered: "you can't work for us and moonlight for the competition."
- The rule is long-standing; you're free to leave and run your own events.
- The FIE isn't worried about local club events. It's worried about rivals to its role as governing body.
- Events run by clubs recognised by your federation (e.g., USA Fencing) are fine under the rule's wording.

**Middle and other views:**
- Having a rule differs from enforcing it, and the FIE is handling this badly.
- The WFL is really just showcase events, not a rival governing body, though some suspect bigger ambitions.
- Comparisons to LIV Golf and the tennis exhibition UTS; doubt the WFL has the money to last.
- Speculation about appeals to CAS or the IOC.
