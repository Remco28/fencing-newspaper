# Controversial Calls: The touch nobody saw land, except the referee

*Editor's note: The fencer in this story is the editor's son.*

**The call.** In a pool bout at a national tournament, an Elite FC fencer's opponent scored a touch that, according to the fencer's family, landed off the strip by accident. The fencer said the blade never hit him. According to the family, the opponent at first said the same, the opponent's coach did not contest it, and the family, the Elite FC coach and other onlookers all saw a miss. The referee awarded the touch; from her angle, the family reports, she either saw it land or could not see otherwise. A more senior referee was called over and let the call stand. The touch cost the fencer the bout. No video replay was available: it was a pool bout.

**The rule.** In épée, the referee must "disregard touches … made on the ground where it is not insulated" (t.93). But whether a touch landed is a "point of fact," which includes "the validity … of a hit," and "no appeal can be made" against it (t.172.1; t.136.2). Appeals are allowed only when a referee misapplies "a definite rule" (t.172.2), and those go to the Head Referee (t.174). The rulebook gives no role to an opponent's admission.

**Both sides (opinion).** For the call: a referee rules on what she sees from her position, and the rules make that final so that bouts can't be re-argued from the stands. Against it: when both fencers and both corners agree on a miss, a call that stands on a limited view feels like the wrong outcome.

**The verdict.** Judgment call. The rules back the referee's right to decide, not necessarily what she saw.

**For parents.** You can't appeal what a referee saw, only how she applied a rule, and only the fencer can make that appeal, right away. At national tournaments, video replay is guaranteed only in the later direct-elimination rounds (from the round of 16 or 8, depending on the event), never in pools. Parent phone video doesn't count.
