**Caption** (43 words)
Nedo Nadi poses in a 1912 Olympic-report portrait, years before his Antwerp team epee gold. That 1920 medal formed part of a five-gold haul. According to the IOC biography, his father had forbidden epee in his fencing school, so Nedo practiced it elsewhere.

**Blurb** (118 words)
Nedo Nadi’s father had a strong opinion about epee: according to the IOC biography, Beppe Nadi called it an “undisciplined” weapon and would not allow it in his school. Nedo practiced elsewhere anyway. The payoff came at Antwerp in 1920, when he helped Italy win Olympic team epee gold. That was one of five gold medals he won at those Games, with victories across all three fencing weapons. This photograph is earlier: a posed portrait catalogued to July 1912 and extracted from the Stockholm official report. It does not show an epee bout. The story offers a lively way to introduce a multiweapon champion while keeping our newsletter’s focus exactly where it belongs: his documented team epee achievement.
