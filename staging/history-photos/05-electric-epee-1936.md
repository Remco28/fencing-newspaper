# 05 · 1936: When epee learned to light up

**Caption** (42 words)
This undated epee from the Museo delle Grigne was photographed in 2016. It accompanies a story from the 1930s, when epee scoring went electric: a surviving newsreel of the 1936 Dutch championship records a jury using an apparatus to signal successful touches.

**Blurb** (101 words)
At the 1936 Dutch national epee championship, the action took place on the terraces of the Kurhaus in Scheveningen. A surviving newsreel describes bouts, spectators on the terrace and a jury with an apparatus that signalled a successful touch; its named winner is W. Driebergen. The equipment belongs to a wider change in the sport. British Fencing dates epee’s electrification to the 1930s and explains why the weapon was comparatively straightforward to electrify: the whole body was target, so the system did not need to distinguish valid from off-target hits. The accompanying museum epee is an undated object photographed in 2016.

**Alt text:** An undated museum epee photographed diagonally on a white background, its straight grip and broad dark bell guard at upper left and its long narrow blade extending to the lower right.
