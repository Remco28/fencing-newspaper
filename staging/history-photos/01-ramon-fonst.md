**Caption** (49 words)
Ramón Fonst appears in a fencing photograph published in June 1900, here cropped from an image with Albert Ayat. At 16, Fonst won Olympic amateur epee gold for Cuba in Paris, followed by silver in a separate masters-and-amateurs event. Those medals were the first for Cuba and Latin America.

**Blurb** (132 words)
Before Cuba became an Olympic fencing name, a teenager helped write its first chapter: Ramón Fonst, born in Havana and trained in France, won the amateur epee competition at Paris 1900 at 16. He also took silver in a separate epee event matching leading amateurs with fencing masters. Those were the first Olympic medals for Cuba and Latin America. This photograph was published in La Vie au Grand Air on 24 June 1900; the supplied crop isolates Fonst from a posed fencing image with Albert Ayat. In 1904, Fonst won Olympic individual epee gold again in St. Louis. He even returned to the Olympics in 1924, after a 20-year absence from the Games. For young Elite FC fencers, it is a reminder that a teenager can belong in a very big story.
