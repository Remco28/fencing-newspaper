Team USA Sweeps Pan-Am Epee Gold in Lima

Comebacks in both team finals cap a six-gold weekend for the Americans in Lima.

Gazette Staff · Epee Only, As Always

The United States won all six team gold medals at the Pan American Senior Zonal Championships in Lima, Peru, with both epee squads delivering the weekend's best drama. The men trailed Venezuela 33–31 after seven legs before Gabriel Feinberg posted a 9–2 eighth leg, and anchor Ariel Simmons held off a 16–5 closing run from Colombia's John Edison Rodriguez to win 45–42. Elijah Imrek and Oleg Knysh fenced steady middle legs in the 14-team field, and the Americans never trailed after the eighth leg.

The women were even more commanding, beating Canada 41–31 behind Hadley Husisian, Catherine Nixon, Charlene Kai Yan Liu, and Tierna Oxenreider. In individual action, Anna van Brummen upset Husisian 15–13 for women's epee gold in a 68-fencer field.

Why it matters here: zonals use the same relay format as our regional events — closing tight legs without panicking is exactly what coaches preach in our room. Feinberg's patience and Simmons holding a shrinking lead are the two hardest things in team fencing. The Gazette will have full individual results in Thursday's edition.

New Passivity Rule Starts Oct 1: Red, Then Black

Under the new rule, stalling no longer earns a warning. The first passivity call goes straight to a red card — a touch for the opponent — and the second to a black card: exclusion from the bout. Yellow cards for passivity are gone entirely. The intent is to keep bouts fencing instead of circling. For our fencers the lesson is simple: always show the referee clear attacking intent, especially late in a period, because the call will now come faster and cost more. Expect referees to enforce it strictly through the fall while everyone adjusts.

Ice Cream Makes Fencers Ten Times Better

A groundbreaking Gazette investigation of the club freezer confirms it: fencers who eat ice cream between periods win ten times as many bouts. Chocolate beats vanilla 15–14 in overtime. Side effects include sticky gloves and questionable footwork. More research — and sprinkles — needed.

Fencing 101 — The Double Touch

Epee has no right-of-way: if both fencers hit within about 40 milliseconds, both score. That's why you see 1–1 counters where foil would give one light. Lesson for beginners: when you're up by one late in a bout, don't chase — make them come to you, because a double keeps your lead. When you're down, avoid the double at all costs; only single-light touches change the math. Tied at the end of a direct-elimination bout, epee goes to a one-minute priority round after a draw for who advances on a tie — win the draw and the pressure flips entirely: your opponent must score, and you only need one light. Body contact is its own lesson: corps-à-corps to avoid a touch is penalized, so keep your distance honest even when the strip gets crowded.
