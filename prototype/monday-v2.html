Team USA Sweeps Pan-Am Epee Gold in Lima

Comebacks in both team finals cap a six-gold weekend for the Americans at the Pan American Zonal Championships.

Gazette Staff · Epee Only, As Always

The United States won all six team gold medals at the Pan American Senior Zonal Championships in Lima, Peru, with both epee squads delivering the weekend's best drama. The men trailed Venezuela 33–31 after seven legs before Gabriel Feinberg posted a 9–2 eighth leg, and anchor Ariel Simmons held off a 16–5 closing run from Colombia's John Edison Rodriguez to win 45–42. Elijah Imrek and Oleg Knysh fenced steady middle legs in the 14-team field.

The women were even more commanding, beating Canada 41–31 behind Hadley Husisian, Catherine Nixon, Charlene Kai Yan Liu, and Tierna Oxenreider. In individual action, Anna van Brummen upset Husisian 15–13 for women's epee gold in a 68-fencer field. The U.S. claimed four of six individual titles overall.

Why it matters here: zonals use the same relay format as our regional events — closing tight legs without panicking is exactly what coaches preach in our room. Feinberg's patience and Simmons holding a shrinking lead are the two hardest things in team fencing.

Fencing 101 — The Double Touch

Epee has no right-of-way: if both fencers hit within about 40 milliseconds, both score. That's why you see 1–1 counters where foil would give one light. Lesson for beginners: when you're up by one late in a bout, don't chase — make them come to you, because a double keeps your lead. When you're down, avoid the double at all costs; only single-light touches change the math. Tied at the end of a direct-elimination bout, epee goes to a one-minute priority round after a draw for who advances on a tie — win the draw and the pressure flips entirely: your opponent must score, and you only need one light.
